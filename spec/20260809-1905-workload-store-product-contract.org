#+title: Workload-oriented log-store product contract
#+date: 2026-08-09 19:05 UTC
#+license: GFDL-1.3-or-later

* Status

Approved for implementation on 2026-08-09. This replaces the operator-facing
and retention portions of =20260809-1626-bounded-replay-log-store.org=.

* Problem

The first bounded-store implementation exposed internal queues, permit pools,
framing limits, and subscriber mechanics as twelve operator settings. That is
an implementation dump, not a usable product contract. It also called a
container stream a source, while the product-facing source is a Kubernetes
workload with identifiable Pod replicas.

* Product vocabulary

- A *workload* is one stable Kubernetes controller identity: API version,
  kind, name, namespace, and UID. Deployment, StatefulSet, DaemonSet, Job, and
  other exactly resolved controllers use their owner UID. A standalone Pod is
  its own workload, keyed by Pod UID.
- A *Pod replica* is one Pod UID belonging to that workload. Current and
  historical replicas share workload retention, but every record preserves its
  Pod UID and Pod name so replay and presentation can separate them honestly.
- A container stream is an internal framing lane. It is not a public source and
  does not receive an independent product retention promise.
- One =LogRecord= belongs to exactly one workload and one Pod when Kubernetes
  identity resolves. Unresolved capture remains explicit and cannot be guessed
  into a workload.

* Operator configuration

The complete store configuration is:

#+begin_src yaml
store:
  max_memory: 128MiB
  max_workloads: 128
  max_logs: 500
  max_record_bytes: 64KiB
#+end_src

- =max_memory= is the hard envelope for memory owned or pinned by the database,
  including retained records and all internal framing, ingress, replay, and
  live-delivery state. It accepts human byte units; operators do not calculate
  queue budgets.
- =max_workloads= is the maximum number of workload identities with retained
  history. It replaces =max_sources= and never means file descriptors,
  containers, or streams.
- =max_logs= is the maximum retained records per workload, shared across all
  current and historical Pod replicas of that workload.
- =max_record_bytes= is the largest single framed record. Larger records are
  explicitly split without silent byte loss.

All queue counts, pending-framer limits, replay admission, subscriber admission,
and subscriber lag bounds are implementation constants or are derived from
=max_memory=. They are not configuration fields. Invalid or impossibly small
memory envelopes fail startup with one actionable error.

The Kubernetes manifest starts with a 64-MiB request and 192-MiB process limit;
the database envelope must leave measured room for eBPF, Tokio, allocator, and
bounded Kubernetes-cache overhead. These values are validated by an RSS test,
not assumed from the sum of logical store counters.

* Retention semantics

Each workload owns one sequence-ordered ring containing records from all of its
Pod replicas. Records retain Pod identity, container identity, and internal
stream provenance, allowing output such as:

#+begin_example
Deployment/game-server (UID d1)
  Pod/game-server-abc (UID p1): records 1, 2, 5
  Pod/game-server-def (UID p2): records 3, 4, 6
#+end_example

The workload ring retains at most =max_logs=. A new record evicts that
workload's oldest record when the count is exceeded. Deleted Pods do not erase
their retained records; deleting or recreating the controller does not merge
different workload UIDs merely because names match.

=max_memory= is also a hard ceiling. Therefore =max_logs= is a count ceiling,
not a promise that every workload can always retain that many arbitrary
maximum-sized records. Those two promises cannot both hold for arbitrary input:

#+begin_example
max_workloads * max_logs * max_record_bytes
#+end_example

can exceed =max_memory=. Under global memory pressure, globally oldest records
are evicted, so a workload may temporarily retain fewer than =max_logs=. The
store reports this reason separately. Subscriber reads never evict or drain
retained history; only count or memory pressure does.

* Subscription semantics

Consumers subscribe to workload, Pod, or namespace identity, never to a file
descriptor. A workload replay includes its retained historical Pod replicas in
store sequence order while preserving Pod identity on every record.

A subscriber has a small private delivery backlog containing shared references
to retained/live records. Receiving a record removes only that delivery
reference. It does not remove the database record. If the consumer cannot keep
up, its internal backlog reaches a constant bound and that connection closes
with =subscriber_lagged=; capture, retention, and other subscribers continue.
The number and backlog size of subscribers remain bounded constants so an
untrusted number of consumers cannot defeat =max_memory=.

* Stream provenance and severity

FD 1/2 classification remains internal capture provenance. It must not define
the public source hierarchy and must not be exposed as though it were durable
stream identity.

=LogRecord= gains a severity field with at least =Unknown=, =Info=, =Warn=, and
=Error=. Severity is set only from explicit evidence after framing, such as a
recognized structured severity field or an approved textual format. FD 2 alone
does not prove =Error=, and FD 1 does not prove =Info=. Unrecognized records are
=Unknown= rather than confidently wrong. The first implementation may leave
all records =Unknown= until a separate classifier contract is approved; the
record model must still carry the field now.

* Internal derivation

One source of truth derives internal bounds from =max_memory=. The derivation:

- reserves fixed headroom for metadata and actor commands;
- gives retained history and one complete replay equal dominant shares;
- bounds aggregate unfinished framing bytes;
- bounds nonblocking ingress by records and bytes;
- bounds all replay pins and all live-delivery references together, not by an
  operator-calculated amount per subscriber; and
- is unit-tested to prove the sum of every owned pool cannot exceed the database
  envelope.

Implementation constants are documented for maintainers and visible in
diagnostics, but absent from the public YAML. Changing the derivation is an
internal performance decision as long as the four public limits and loss
semantics remain true.

* Migration

- Replace the twelve-field =StoreConfig= with the four-field product contract.
- Reject removed fields with a concise migration error rather than silently
  accepting ignored limits.
- Re-key retention from container-stream leaves to workload UID rings while
  preserving Pod/container/provenance metadata on each record.
- Keep internal framing lanes keyed finely enough that bytes from different
  containers or streams can never be joined into a fabricated line.
- Replace exact internal-source subscription with workload/Pod/namespace
  selectors.
- Rename metrics and diagnostics from source counts to workload and Pod terms.
- Update the manifest, field guide, roadmap, benchmarks, and retention/replay
  model tests.

* Acceptance

- Public YAML has exactly the four store settings above.
- No public type or diagnostic calls an FD/container stream a source.
- One workload never retains more than =max_logs= records across all replicas.
- Historical Pod replicas remain queryable and visibly distinct within their
  workload until their records are evicted.
- Subscriber consumption never changes retained-record counts.
- Every internal pool is included in the =max_memory= derivation and tested.
- The configured database envelope and process memory limit pass burst/RSS
  validation on the deployment workload.
- Severity is evidence-based and permits =Unknown=; no stdout/stderr guess is
  presented as fact.
