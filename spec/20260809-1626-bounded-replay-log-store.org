#+title: Bounded in-memory log store and gap-free replay handoff
#+date: 2026-08-09 16:26 UTC
#+license: GFDL-1.3-or-later

* Status

Superseded on 2026-08-09 by =20260809-1905-workload-store-product-contract.org=
for public configuration, source vocabulary, selectors, and retention. Its
framing and replay-watermark mechanics remain historical design context only.

* Goal

Keep a bounded, node-local history of accepted logs and expose one internal
subscription operation that atomically returns retained history followed by
new matching records. Capture and pipeline work must continue independently of
store and subscriber speed.

This is volatile process memory. Restarting boring-logtail forgets records,
sequence numbers, and subscriptions.

* Existing boundaries

The current userspace path is:

#+begin_example
WriteEvent chunks
  -> WriteAssembler
  -> WriteOperation
  -> enrich, filter, sample, classify
  -> ProcessedWrite
  -> temporary human output
#+end_example

=ProcessedWrite= is one completed syscall write, not necessarily one
application log line. The store must consume a new immutable =LogRecord=
boundary after explicit userspace framing; it must not rename syscall writes
as logs. The pipeline remains the only publisher of accepted records.

At the time of this design, Kubernetes identity had no workload owner. That gap
motivated explicit owner discovery rather than name-based guesses; the
superseding contract now uses the resolved workload UID.

* Historical framing and replay design

The product vocabulary, limits, retention key, and selectors in the original
version of this section are superseded. The current contract is
=20260809-1905-workload-store-product-contract.org=: a workload-UID ring,
aggregate =max_logs= across current and historical Pod replicas, preserved Pod
identity, workload/Pod/namespace selectors, and a 64-KiB default record limit.
Container streams are internal framing lanes, not retention sources.

** Log framing

The framing mechanics remain useful historical context. =RecordFramer= is
byte-preserving newline framing per internal container-stream lane:

- concatenate accepted =ProcessedWrite.operation.bytes= in pipeline order for
  one lane;
- emit through and including each newline as one =LogRecord=;
- keep an unterminated suffix for the next write;
- cap one record and pending suffix at the configured limit; crossing the cap
  emits a record fragment marked =max_size_split= and continues without losing
  bytes;
- bound aggregate pending bytes internally; pressure emits
  the oldest suffix marked =capacity_split= rather than growing or dropping it;
- carry first and last capture timestamps and explicit capture-integrity flags;
- never require UTF-8, JSON, or multiline interpretation; severity remains
  =Unknown= without explicit evidence; and
- expose an explicit shutdown/lane-removal flush for an unterminated suffix,
  marked =unterminated=. A crash may lose that pending suffix because this
  design has no persistence.

Sampling currently happens per write before this future framing boundary. That
would manufacture incomplete lines whenever only part of a multi-write line is
sampled. This design therefore moves sampling after framing:

#+begin_example
assemble -> enrich -> source filter -> classify -> frame -> sample -> publish
#+end_example

Sampling makes one decision per complete or explicitly split =LogRecord=.
=OutputPolicy= must derive its deterministic decision from stable record facts
rather than one =WriteOperation=. One input write can complete zero, one, or
many records, so the pipeline output contract must support a bounded batch.
Source filters remain before framing because they decide whether the source is
admitted; content-aware filters remain future work after framing. Any upstream
capture loss still appears in record integrity and must not be presented as a
complete stream. Read-failed chunks split records at explicit =capture_gap=
boundaries, including a visible zero-byte loss record when no payload could be
copied.

** Superseded retention identity

The original leaf-source key is not the product retention key. Retention now
belongs to the resolved workload UID, with Pod and container identity retained
on each record. Unknown or cyclic owner chains remain explicit and do not fall
back to names.

** Stored record

Each accepted =LogRecord= receives one process-local monotonically increasing
=StoreSequence=. Stored payload bytes are immutable and shared between the
store, replay snapshots, and live delivery. Sequence order, not kernel
timestamp order, defines store and subscription order.

* Ownership and transport

One Tokio task exclusively owns all mutable store state:

#+begin_example
capture task -> Pipeline -> RecordFramer -> bounded ingress channel
                                             |
                                             v
                                      LogStore owner task
                                             |
                              replay snapshot + bounded live receiver
                                             |
                                             v
                                       streaming adapter
#+end_example

The owner handles =Append=, =Subscribe=, and shutdown commands. Dropping the
live receiver is unsubscribe; its global subscriber-slot permit remains held
until that receiver is actually dropped.
No caller receives mutable store internals. The capture task uses non-blocking
send so a full store-ingress channel cannot stop draining the eBPF ring buffer.
Ingress overflow drops the record with a distinct counter and integrity/loss
diagnostic; it is never reported as sampling or kernel-ring loss.

The initial implementation uses the standard =HashMap=, =VecDeque=, and shared
immutable byte ownership. It adds no generic storage trait and no custom
allocator.

* Superseded capacity and eviction contract

The original pool-by-pool defaults are not operator configuration. Operators
configure only =max_memory=, =max_workloads=, =max_logs=, and
=max_record_bytes=; internal framing, ingress, replay, and subscriber bounds are
derived from =max_memory=. =max_record_bytes= defaults to 64 KiB.

On append, the workload's oldest record is evicted when aggregate =max_logs=
across its Pod replicas is exceeded. Global memory pressure evicts globally
oldest records. Replay references can pin shared records, but subscriber reads
release only delivery references and never drain retained history.

* Subscription contract

A selector targets workload UID, Pod UID, or namespace. Broad replay is ordered
by =StoreSequence=. Exact container-stream selection from the original design
is superseded.

The owner processes subscription atomically with appends:

1. choose watermark =N=, the last assigned store sequence;
2. collect all matching retained records with sequence =<= N=;
3. reserve replay memory and register the live subscriber;
4. return =ReplaySession { through: N, replay, live_receiver }=; and
5. route only matching records with sequence => N= to that receiver.

The adapter sends replay in sequence order before reading live delivery. This
guarantees no gap and no duplicate at the replay/live boundary while the
session remains connected. It does not claim durable exactly-once delivery:
there are no acknowledgements, reconnect cursors, or persisted sequences.

Subscribers never apply backpressure to capture or the store owner. If either
the live record or byte limit is exceeded while replay or network sending is
slow, close that subscription with an explicit =subscriber_lagged= terminal
reason and counter. Silently dropping selected records while leaving the
subscription open is forbidden.

* Workload ownership

The node-local Pod watch is the source for Pod owner references, but a direct
owner is not always the workload a human asks for. Workload indexing must:

- use owner UIDs, kinds, and API versions rather than labels or generated names;
- resolve at least direct Pod owners and ReplicaSet -> Deployment chains;
- preserve the last known owner with the same stale semantics as Pod identity;
- bound all owner caches and API work outside the event path; and
- expose unresolved and unsupported chains rather than guessing.

The exact set of supported controller chains must be documented from fixtures.
Workload subscriptions are not enabled until their chain is supported.
The bounded Pod-UID history preserves a last resolved Deployment when its
ReplicaSet temporarily disappears from the owner cache and marks that snapshot
stale; deleting the Pod removes the history.

* Observability

Keep distinct totals for accepted records, workload-count eviction,
global-budget eviction, workload-limit rejection, ingress overflow, replay
admission rejection, subscriber lag, explicit unsubscribe, and shutdown flush.
Report retained records, retained bytes, workload count, subscriber count, and
pinned replay bytes as bounded gauges or periodic diagnostics.

No payload bytes, credentials, or arbitrary selector values are written to
diagnostics.

* Allocator decision

A self-cooked arena is not an architectural requirement. Variable payloads,
workload eviction, and subscriber-held snapshots make reclamation the hard part,
not allocation itself.

Benchmark the standard-library baseline first. Prototype a generation/slab
allocator only if allocation and reclamation consume at least 10% of store CPU
at the validated workload. Adopt it only if it improves p99 append latency or
sustained throughput by at least 15% without weakening byte bounds, increasing
pinned memory, or introducing unsafe code without a separate review. Remove
the prototype otherwise.

The 2026-08-09 release-mode baseline ran all 27 combinations of 64-byte, 1-KiB,
and 16-KiB payloads; 100, 1,000, and 4,096 sources; and 0, 1, and 64 live
subscribers. Representative results:

| Payload | Sources | Subscribers | records/s | p50 append | p99 append | allocations/10k | allocated/10k | cumulative VmHWM |
|-
| 64 B | 100 | 0 | 403,438 | 320 ns | 6,660 ns | 254,470 | 14.3 MB | 16,540 KiB |
| 64 B | 4,096 | 64 | 193,727 | 1,130 ns | 9,140 ns | 339,870 | 26.9 MB | 44,780 KiB |
| 1 KiB | 4,096 | 64 | 182,492 | 950 ns | 8,620 ns | 339,604 | 26.7 MB | 45,016 KiB |
| 16 KiB | 4,096 | 0 | 431,358 | 1,010 ns | 5,910 ns | 354,271 | 26.5 MB | 103,144 KiB |
| 16 KiB | 4,096 | 64 | 197,184 | 690 ns | 6,490 ns | 338,626 | 26.0 MB | 115,108 KiB |

=stats_alloc= measured process-wide allocations during publication; payload
allocation happened before the timed store handoff, matching the store's owned
input boundary. =VmHWM= is cumulative across the matrix, not isolated per row.
No CPU profiler is installed in this environment, so allocation/reclamation
was not shown to consume the required 10% of store CPU. The approved decision
is therefore to keep ordinary safe allocation and not build an arena.

* Non-goals

- Persistence, cross-node history, replication, or recovery after restart.
- A public HTTP, gRPC, WebSocket, authentication, or authorization contract.
- Durable exactly-once delivery, acknowledgements, or reconnect cursors.
- Severity inference, regex, or semantic multiline parsing. Records carry
  severity, but it remains =Unknown= absent explicit evidence.
- Pretending fd 1/2 is durable stream identity before descriptor tracking lands.
- Keeping a second ring per namespace, workload, or Pod.
- Introducing an arena before measurements justify one.
